# Framing

Framing creates the building skeleton: straight, strong, serviceable, dry, and coordinated with every trade that follows. The best framing is not just code-minimum; it gives drywall, cabinets, tile, windows, siding, roofing, MEP, and trim a clean substrate.

## Core Priorities

1. Continuous load path from roof to foundation.
2. Proper bearing at beams, headers, joists, rafters, posts, and point loads.
3. Straight, plumb, square, level, and flat enough for intended finishes.
4. Correct fastening and connectors.
5. Proper sheathing layout, panel gaps, and nailing.
6. Coordination for plumbing, electrical, HVAC, insulation, air sealing, and fireblocking.

## Layout

- Establish control lines from reliable benchmarks, not from old crooked walls or slab edges.
- Check square with diagonals, laser, or 3-4-5/6-8-10 method.
- Mark plates together where possible.
- Mark studs, openings, jacks, kings, cripples, posts, beam pockets, hold-downs, wall intersections, blocking, and backing.
- Keep stud layout consistent with sheathing and drywall breaks.
- Use a story pole for window heads, sill heights, door heads, cabinet blocking, switches, wainscot, and trim transitions.

## Lumber and Engineered Products

### Dimensional Lumber

- Species and grade matter. Do not substitute lower-grade lumber where plans specify a grade/species.
- Crown joists and rafters consistently upward unless engineered design says otherwise.
- Cull or relocate twisted, split, crushed, wet, badly crowned, or checked stock.
- Use the best studs at doors, windows, cabinet walls, tall walls, tile walls, stair walls, and long trim runs.

### Engineered Wood

- LVL, PSL, LSL, glulam, I-joists, rim board, floor trusses, and roof trusses are engineered systems.
- Do not notch, bore, rip, taper, or field-modify engineered members unless manufacturer literature or an engineer allows it.
- I-joist holes must follow the manufacturer hole chart.
- Trusses must not be cut or altered in the field without a truss repair detail.

## Sill Plates and Foundation Interface

- Use pressure-treated or approved decay-resistant sill material where wood contacts concrete or masonry.
- Install sill sealer or capillary break as required and to reduce air leakage.
- Anchor bolts, plate washers, straps, and hold-downs must match drawings/code.
- Verify anchor bolt layout before wall framing; bolts in stud, jack, or hold-down locations create problems.
- Shim plate gaps with non-compressible material where bearing is required.
- Check foundation square, elevation, diagonals, step-downs, beam pockets, and slab depressions before framing.

## Wall Framing

### Stud Walls

- Standard layouts are commonly 16 inches or 24 inches on center, but use the project design.
- Exterior walls must satisfy gravity, wind, bracing/shear, insulation, and cladding needs.
- Interior walls need proper backing, fireblocking, sound control, and MEP access even if nonbearing.
- Align studs over joists or blocking when required by load path or detail.

### Corners and Intersections

- Provide drywall backing without creating unnecessary thermal bridges.
- California corners and ladder blocking can improve insulation continuity.
- Add backing for cabinets, handrails, grab bars, towel bars, TV mounts, barn doors, closet systems, stair skirts, built-ins, and wall-hung fixtures.

### Openings

- Header sizing depends on span, load width, snow/roof/floor loads, building width, species, grade, and bearing.
- Exterior and bearing-wall openings usually require sized headers.
- King studs run full height beside openings.
- Jack/trimmer studs support the header.
- Cripples above/below openings should align with layout where practical.
- Rough openings must fit the unit, shim space, flashing, and manufacturer clearance.

### Bearing and Point Loads

- Posts under beams must bear on adequate support below, not just subfloor.
- Stack load paths through floors wherever possible.
- Do not allow a beam, girder truss, or concentrated load to land on unsupported framing.
- Verify post base, cap, straps, hold-downs, and uplift hardware where wind/seismic loads apply.

## Floor Framing

### Joists

- Joist depth, spacing, span, species, grade, and deflection limit must match plans/span tables.
- Provide full bearing or approved hangers.
- Use proper hanger nails/screws, not generic fasteners.
- Install blocking/bridging where required for roll resistance, load sharing, and panel edge support.
- Watch deflection for tile, stone, long-span floors, tubs, islands, and heavy partitions.

### Holes and Notches

- Avoid notching joists unless allowed by code/manufacturer.
- Do not notch the middle third of dimensional joist spans.
- Keep holes away from top/bottom edges and supports per applicable rules.
- Never cut flanges of I-joists.
- Protect bored studs/plates with nail plates where pipes or cables are close to the face.

### Subfloor

- Use correct panel thickness, span rating, exposure rating, and orientation.
- Install panels with strength axis perpendicular to joists unless product/design says otherwise.
- Leave required panel expansion gaps.
- Use rated subfloor adhesive when specified or as good practice for squeak control.
- Fasten to schedule; avoid overdriven fasteners.
- Sand swollen panel joints before flooring.

## Roof Framing

### Rafters

- Rafter sizing depends on pitch, span, spacing, species/grade, roof load, snow load, and ceiling load.
- A ridge board aligns rafters; a ridge beam carries vertical roof load and needs engineered support.
- Birdsmouth cuts must leave adequate rafter depth.
- Rafter ties resist outward wall thrust; collar ties are higher and address separation/uplift depending on design.
- Hips, valleys, dormers, and intersecting roofs create concentrated loads that need proper bearing.

### Trusses

- Install per truss layout and bracing package.
- Do not cut webs, chords, or plates.
- Temporary bracing is critical during setting; permanent bracing must be installed per design.
- Truss uplift can crack drywall; use proper clips/details where specified.
- Damaged trusses require repair details from the truss designer/engineer.

## Sheathing and Bracing

- Sheathing can be structural, weather-related, or both.
- Use the specified panel rating, thickness, nailing, edge support, and orientation.
- Stagger joints where required.
- Keep panel edges gapped as manufacturer requires.
- Do not overdrive nails in shear walls/diaphragms.
- Braced wall lines, portal frames, hold-downs, and shear panels are code/engineer-sensitive; follow drawings.

## Fireblocking and Draftstopping

Common fireblocking locations:

- Top and bottom of concealed wall cavities.
- Floor-to-wall intersections.
- Soffits, dropped ceilings, chases, furred spaces, and stair cavities.
- Around ducts, pipes, vents, wires, chimneys, and fireplaces.
- At concealed vertical-to-horizontal transitions.

Use approved materials and listed sealants where required. Do not assume insulation alone is approved fireblocking.

## Framing for Other Trades

- Plumbing needs properly sized walls, chases, vent paths, shower valve backing, tub/deck support, and drain slope.
- Electrical needs box layout, switch legs, panel clearance, nail protection, and fixture backing.
- HVAC needs duct chases, returns, soffits, equipment platforms, and access panels.
- Drywall needs backing at corners, ceiling transitions, tubs, soffits, tray ceilings, and stair openings.
- Exterior cladding needs sheathing, WRB, flashing support, furring, and clearances.

## Common Failure Modes

- Walls framed out of plumb, making cabinets, doors, tile, and trim difficult.
- Point loads not carried to foundation.
- Oversized holes/notches in studs, joists, rafters, or engineered lumber.
- Missing blocking at panel edges, cabinets, rails, or fixtures.
- Overdriven shear-wall nails.
- Trusses cut by other trades.
- Wet framing enclosed too soon.
- Missing fireblocking in chases and soffits.
- Rough openings too tight for flashing and shimming.

## Field QA Checklist

Before inspection or cover-up:

- Foundation, sill, anchor bolts, and hold-downs verified.
- Walls plumb, straight, square, and braced.
- Headers sized and bearing properly.
- Posts stacked and supported below.
- Joists/rafters/trusses installed per layout.
- Sheathing nailed to schedule with correct gaps.
- Hardware installed with correct fasteners.
- Fireblocking complete.
- Backing installed for fixtures, cabinets, rails, and trim.
- MEP penetrations protected and within allowed limits.
- Photos taken before insulation/drywall/siding.
