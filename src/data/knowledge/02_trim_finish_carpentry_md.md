# Trim and Finish Carpentry

Finish carpentry hides nothing. It exposes framing quality, drywall quality, floor flatness, door setting, humidity control, and layout discipline. Good trim is planned before drywall and executed after the building is dry, conditioned, and stable.

## Core Priorities

1. Straight reveals and intentional shadow lines.
2. Tight joints where they matter; controlled movement where materials need to move.
3. Proper substrate: flat, dry, backed, and fastenable.
4. Consistent layout around doors, windows, stairs, cabinets, and flooring.
5. Durable fastening, glue strategy, caulk strategy, and finish prep.

## Pre-Trim Conditions

- Building should be dried-in and conditioned when possible.
- Drywall should be finished, sanded, primed where appropriate, and repaired before trim.
- Flooring sequence must be known: trim over finished floor, undercut jambs, base shoe, stair nosing, transitions.
- Cabinet and built-in layout should be coordinated before casing/base conflicts occur.
- Check walls for bows, proud drywall, bad corner bead, out-of-plane jambs, and high floor spots.
- Acclimate wood trim to site conditions.

## Material Basics

### Solid Wood

- Moves across grain with humidity.
- Best for stain-grade, high-end paint-grade, and profiles needing crisp machining.
- Back-prime or seal exterior and high-moisture trim where appropriate.
- Watch cupping, twist, checking, and grain mismatch.

### MDF

- Stable and smooth for paint-grade interior work.
- Poor choice for wet areas, exterior, and abuse-prone bases unless specifically rated.
- Edges need sealing/priming.
- Fasteners can mushroom; predrill or use sharp finish nails where needed.

### PVC/Composite Trim

- Good exterior rot resistance but moves with temperature.
- Requires manufacturer-specific expansion gaps, fastening, adhesives, and paint color limits.
- Use compatible sealants and fasteners.

### Finger-Jointed/Painted Trim

- Economical and stable for paint-grade.
- Avoid exposing unsealed ends to moisture.
- Inspect for telegraphing joints, poor primer, and defects.

## Doors

### Prehung Door Setting

- Verify rough opening: width, height, plumb, square, and floor level.
- Set hinge side first; make it plumb and straight.
- Shim behind hinges and strike area.
- Use long screws into framing at hinges where needed, especially heavy doors.
- Maintain even reveal around head and strike side.
- Do not force a door to fit a bad opening; correct or shim intentionally.
- Confirm latch, strike, swing, clearance, and floor finish height before casing.

### Common Door Problems

- Door swings open/closed: hinge jamb out of plumb.
- Uneven head reveal: jamb legs not parallel, floor out of level, or head out of level.
- Rub at top corner: hinge jamb twisted, slab warped, or frame racked.
- Latch misses strike: strike jamb bowed, wrong reveal, or slab sag.
- Split jamb movement: poor shimming or fastening.

## Window and Door Casing

- Establish reveal with a gauge block; do not eyeball.
- Common reveal is around 3/16 inch, but match design and existing work.
- Use backcuts or slight bevels for tight miters on imperfect walls.
- Glue casing miters; pin/nail to hold alignment.
- Cope or miter depending on profile, paint/stain, movement, and site standard.
- Use extension jambs where drywall depth varies.
- For stain-grade, grain, color, and miter fit matter more than caulkability.

## Baseboard and Shoe

- Snap or laser reference lines when floors vary.
- Scribe base to uneven floors for high-end work; use shoe/base cap where design allows.
- Cope inside corners for profiled base, especially on long runs and seasonal movement.
- Scarf long runs with bevels at studs, glue, and pin as needed.
- Nail into studs/bottom plate, not just drywall.
- Keep base height coordinated with cabinets, door casing blocks, stair skirts, and transitions.

## Crown Molding

- Establish spring angle and keep it consistent.
- Use backing strips or blocking for reliable fastening.
- Cope inside corners unless the site standard says otherwise.
- Miter outside corners carefully; out-of-square rooms require measured angles.
- Long runs should be scarfed and glued.
- Crown reveals ceiling/wall waves; plan for caulk lines or correct substrate first.
- For large crown, preassemble outside corners when possible.

## Wainscot, Paneling, and Feature Walls

- Layout from visible centerlines and focal walls, not simply from one corner.
- Keep panel widths balanced around doors, windows, corners, and outlets.
- Plan electrical boxes before installing rails/stiles.
- Use level lines, story poles, and spacers.
- Use adhesive selectively; allow future repair where practical.
- Back-prime or seal panels in moisture-variable rooms.

## Built-Ins, Cabinets, and Shelving

- Find and mark studs before installation.
- Shim cabinets level, plumb, and flush before fastening.
- Fasten through structural hanging rails, not thin backs alone.
- Scribe fillers to walls and ceilings.
- Plan reveal consistency at face frames, fillers, end panels, toe kicks, and crown.
- Shelves need span-appropriate thickness or support to avoid sag.
- In wet/utility rooms, use moisture-resistant materials and finish all cut edges.

## Stairs, Rails, and Guards

- Stairs and guards are life-safety work. Verify local code, plans, and inspection requirements.
- Tread/riser consistency is critical; small variations cause trips and inspection failures.
- Handrails need proper height, graspability, returns, continuity, and fastening.
- Guard infill spacing and height are code-sensitive.
- Newel posts must be structurally fastened; trim-only fastening is not enough.
- Stair skirts require careful scribing; do not hide unsafe geometry with trim.

## Exterior Trim

- Maintain drainage and drying. Do not create water traps.
- Back-prime or seal cut ends where required.
- Use corrosion-resistant fasteners compatible with trim and treated lumber.
- Flash horizontal trim, band boards, belly bands, and cap details.
- Leave required gaps at dissimilar materials and seal with compatible sealant.
- PVC/composite trim requires expansion planning.
- Do not rely on caulk as the primary flashing.

## Fastening and Adhesives

- Use nails long enough to reach framing where needed.
- 15ga/16ga nails are common for casing/base; 18ga/pins are for small profiles and holding parts.
- Glue miters, returns, and assembled corners where appropriate.
- Construction adhesive can help but can also make repairs destructive.
- Use stainless or approved exterior fasteners outside.
- Fill nail holes with product suited to paint or stain finish.

## Caulk and Finish Prep

- Caulk is for small paint-grade gaps, not poor joinery or failed flashing.
- Use paintable flexible caulk at wall/trim joints.
- Do not caulk stain-grade joints unless specified.
- Prime cut ends and raw MDF edges.
- Sand filled holes flush; avoid shiny smears on primed trim.
- Keep dust control tight before finish coats.

## Tolerances and Visual Standards

- Reveals should be consistent even if walls are not perfect.
- A slightly wider intentional reveal often looks better than a tight reveal that waves.
- Align head casing across adjacent doors/windows when possible.
- Long sight lines matter: hall base, stair skirts, crown, cabinet fillers, and window stools.
- Mock up unusual profiles or transitions before committing.

## Common Failure Modes

- Trim installed before moisture/humidity stabilizes.
- Casing reveals inconsistent due to uncorrected jambs.
- Base follows bad floor waves without intentional scribing or shoe.
- Crown nailed only to drywall.
- Exterior horizontal trim installed without flashing.
- MDF used in wet or exterior locations.
- Caulk used to hide structural movement or water-management failure.
- Cabinet fillers not scribed, leaving tapered gaps.

## Field QA Checklist

Before calling trim complete:

- Doors swing, latch, and reveal correctly.
- Casing reveals consistent.
- Base joints tight, coped/mitred cleanly, fastened to framing.
- Crown spring angle and corners consistent.
- Nail holes filled and sanded.
- Caulk neat and paintable.
- Exterior trim sealed/flashed/draining.
- Built-ins anchored and scribed.
- Stair/rail/guard work checked against code and drawings.
- Finish touch-up list documented before final paint.
