# Roofing

Roofing is a water-management system, not just a finished surface. Most roof failures come from poor flashing, bad sequencing, weak ventilation strategy, wrong fasteners, damaged underlayment, or relying on sealant where lapped flashing should do the work.

## Core Priorities

1. Shed bulk water quickly.
2. Lap every layer shingle-style from low to high.
3. Flash transitions, penetrations, valleys, walls, chimneys, skylights, and edges correctly.
4. Keep roof deck solid, dry, and properly fastened.
5. Provide ventilation when the assembly requires it.
6. Use manufacturer-specific installation details.
7. Protect workers from falls.

## Roof Deck Inspection

Before roofing:

- Check sheathing thickness, span rating, fastening, moisture damage, rot, delamination, and soft spots.
- Replace damaged panels; do not roof over bad deck.
- Verify deck is flat enough for the product.
- Check attic ventilation pathways, baffles, insulation contact, and air sealing.
- Confirm roof slope is acceptable for the selected roofing system.
- Verify drip edge, fascia, rake, gutter, and wall transitions.

## Underlayment and Ice/Water Protection

- Underlayment is secondary drainage, not a substitute for roofing.
- Install from eave upward with proper laps.
- Keep underlayment tight, flat, and undamaged.
- Ice/water membrane is commonly used at eaves, valleys, roof-to-wall intersections, penetrations, and other vulnerable areas, but local code and climate determine requirements.
- In cold regions, eave membrane often needs to extend inside the exterior wall line; verify local code.
- Synthetic underlayments have specific exposure limits, fastener requirements, and slip hazards.

## Drip Edge and Edges

- Drip edge helps protect deck edges, fascia, and underlayment laps.
- Typical sequencing: eave drip edge often goes under underlayment; rake drip edge often goes over underlayment. Verify local code and manufacturer.
- Keep starter courses aligned and properly sealed.
- At gutters, ensure water sheds into gutter without backing behind fascia.

## Asphalt Shingles

### Installation Basics

- Use shingles on slopes approved by manufacturer and code.
- Follow exact exposure, offset, nail placement, and fastening pattern.
- Nails should be driven flush, not underdriven, angled, or overdriven.
- High-wind zones may require enhanced nailing patterns and starter/edge details.
- Use starter strips at eaves and rakes where required.
- Keep bundles mixed to avoid color striping.
- Avoid roofing over wet, rotten, or uneven decking.

### Common Shingle Failures

- Nails too high or overdriven.
- Missing starter course.
- Bad valley method.
- No kickout flashing at roof-wall-gutter intersections.
- Pipe boots cracked or improperly installed.
- Reused flashing that no longer laps correctly.
- Insufficient attic ventilation causing heat/moisture damage.
- Sealant used as primary water defense.

## Valleys

Valleys collect heavy water flow. Treat them as high-risk areas.

- Keep valley substrate smooth and solid.
- Use valley lining required by manufacturer/code.
- Do not place fasteners in the water channel.
- Maintain straight, clean valley lines.
- Woven, closed-cut, and open-metal valleys each have product-specific rules.
- At dead valleys or low-slope crickets, consider membrane and drainage design carefully.

## Roof-to-Wall Flashing

### Step Flashing

- Step flashing should be integrated with each shingle course.
- Each piece laps the piece below and is covered by siding, counterflashing, or wall cladding detail.
- Do not replace step flashing with a continuous bead of caulk.
- Avoid face-fastening exposed flashing where movement and leakage can occur.

### Kickout Flashing

- Use kickout flashing where a sloped roof terminates into a sidewall and gutter/eave area.
- Kickout directs water away from the wall cladding and into the gutter.
- Missing kickouts are a common cause of hidden wall rot.

## Chimneys, Skylights, and Large Penetrations

- Use base flashing, step flashing, head flashing, and counterflashing as appropriate.
- Chimneys on upper roof slopes may need crickets/saddles depending on width and code/design.
- Skylights require manufacturer-specific flashing kits and curb/deck details.
- Avoid relying on tar, mastic, or sealant as the primary long-term waterproofing.
- Verify clearances to combustibles and chimney condition.

## Pipe Boots, Vents, and Small Penetrations

- Use properly sized flashing boots.
- Shingle the flange into the roofing, leaving only the lower portion exposed as designed.
- Keep nails out of exposed water paths where possible.
- Replace cracked rubber boots; do not depend on surface caulk.
- Group penetrations thoughtfully to reduce roof clutter and flashing risk.

## Metal Roofing Basics

- Metal roofing is system-specific: exposed fastener, standing seam, snap lock, mechanical seam, etc.
- Panel length, expansion, clip spacing, fastener type, underlayment, slope, and trim details are manufacturer-specific.
- Cut edges and dissimilar metals can corrode.
- Avoid untreated sawdust/metal shavings left on panels; they can rust-stain.
- Pay close attention to eaves, rakes, hips, ridges, valleys, end laps, and penetrations.
- Snow retention may be required above entries, walks, lower roofs, and mechanical equipment.

## Low-Slope Roofing Awareness

- Low-slope roofs are membrane systems, not shingle roofs.
- TPO, PVC, EPDM, modified bitumen, and built-up systems need correct substrate, slope, drains, scuppers, edge metal, terminations, and penetrations.
- Ponding water is a design and maintenance issue.
- Do not use standard asphalt shingles below their approved slope.

## Attic Ventilation and Condensation

- Ventilation strategy depends on roof assembly: vented attic, unvented conditioned attic, cathedral ceiling, hot roof, or insulated deck.
- Common vented assemblies need clear intake at soffit/eave and exhaust near ridge/high points.
- Baffles keep insulation from blocking airflow.
- Air sealing at ceiling penetrations is as important as vent area for condensation control.
- Bath fans, dryer vents, and kitchen exhaust should discharge outdoors, not into attics.

## Reroofing Considerations

- Verify local rules on number of layers.
- Tear-off exposes deck condition and allows proper flashing replacement.
- Do not trap old leaks or rotten sheathing under new roofing.
- Replace or properly integrate flashing; old flashing is often the weak link.
- Check attic for stains, mold, rusted nails, daylight, and ventilation problems.

## Roofing Coordination

- Gutters and downspouts need discharge paths away from foundation.
- Siding and roofing overlap at step flashing; coordinate sequencing.
- Solar, satellite, HVAC, plumbing vents, and roof accessories should use flashed mounts.
- Masonry repairs and chimney caps should be completed before final roofing where possible.

## Common Leak Diagnostics

Water often appears indoors away from the actual leak. Check uphill first.

- Roof-to-wall intersections.
- Kickout flashing areas.
- Valleys and dead valleys.
- Chimneys and skylights.
- Pipe boots and vent flashings.
- Ridge/hip cap failures.
- Ice dams at eaves.
- Wind-driven rain under damaged shingles.
- Condensation mistaken for a roof leak.

## Field QA Checklist

Before calling roof complete:

- Deck sound and fastened.
- Underlayment and ice/water protection installed correctly.
- Drip edge installed and lapped properly.
- Valleys clean and fastener-free in water channel.
- Step flashing and kickouts installed.
- Penetrations properly flashed.
- Ridge/hip caps installed to spec.
- Attic ventilation path verified.
- Debris, nails, and metal shavings cleaned up.
- Photos taken of flashing before siding/trim covers it.
