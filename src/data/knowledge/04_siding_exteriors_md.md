# Siding and Exterior Envelope

Siding is not the primary waterproofing layer by itself. A durable wall assembly manages water with cladding, flashing, water-resistive barrier, drainage, drying, air sealing, and proper clearances.

## Core Priorities

1. Keep bulk water out.
2. Drain incidental water that gets behind cladding.
3. Let the assembly dry.
4. Maintain required clearances from grade, roofs, decks, concrete, masonry, and paving.
5. Integrate windows, doors, penetrations, and roofs with the WRB.
6. Use compatible fasteners, sealants, metals, and tapes.
7. Follow manufacturer installation instructions for the actual product.

## Wall Layers

A typical framed wall exterior may include:

- Structural sheathing.
- Water-resistive barrier, housewrap, building paper, or fluid-applied membrane.
- Flashing tape and metal/head flashings.
- Rainscreen/furring or drainage mat where required or preferred.
- Cladding and trim.
- Sealant at selected joints, not as the primary drainage system.

## Water-Resistive Barrier

- WRB should be continuous behind siding and integrated with flashing.
- Lap upper layers over lower layers shingle-style.
- Repair tears, cuts, reverse laps, and unsealed penetrations.
- Do not trap water behind reverse-lapped tape or poorly integrated housewrap.
- Use cap nails/cap staples where required by product and wind exposure.
- Maintain continuity at corners, decks, ledgers, roof-wall intersections, and foundation transitions.

## Flashing Openings

Window and door flashing must match the unit, flange type, wall system, and WRB method.

General principles:

- Sill protection first, with a drainage path to the exterior.
- Side flashing laps over sill flashing.
- Head flashing/drip cap laps over side flashing.
- WRB above laps over head flashing.
- Do not seal the bottom flange of some flanged windows when the manufacturer's drainage method requires it open.
- Pan flashing is preferred for high-exposure openings.

## Roof-Wall and Deck-Wall Interfaces

- Step flashing and kickout flashing must be integrated behind siding and over the roof drainage plane.
- Leave siding clearance above roof surfaces per product requirements.
- Deck ledgers need proper flashing, spacing/drainage, corrosion-resistant fasteners, and structural attachment.
- Avoid trapping water behind ledger boards, band boards, belly bands, and horizontal trim.

## Clearances

Verify product-specific requirements. Common clearance concerns include:

- Siding too close to soil/grade.
- Siding buried by mulch.
- Siding tight to roof shingles.
- Siding tight to concrete patios, steps, or driveways.
- Wood or fiber-cement trim touching masonry or concrete without clearance/protection.
- No drainage gap above horizontal flashing.

## Rainscreens and Drainage Gaps

- A rainscreen is a drainage and drying space behind cladding.
- Especially useful in wet climates, reservoir claddings, thick walls, high-performance walls, and coastal exposure.
- Furring strips should align with studs or provide adequate fastening.
- Maintain insect screening at top/bottom where needed without blocking drainage.
- Coordinate rainscreen depth with windows, doors, trim, and exterior outlets.

## Fasteners and Corrosion

- Use fasteners approved by the siding manufacturer.
- Treated lumber, coastal exposure, masonry, and dissimilar metals can accelerate corrosion.
- Stainless steel is often preferred in severe exposure.
- Do not overdrive fasteners in products that need movement.
- Hidden fastener systems still need correct layout and substrate.

## Caulking and Sealants

- Sealant is maintenance, not a permanent structural or waterproofing substitute.
- Use sealant compatible with siding, trim, WRB, flashing, paint, and expected movement.
- Tool sealant to proper shape; deep joints may need backer rod.
- Leave weep/drainage openings open where the system requires them.
- Do not caulk horizontal laps designed to drain.

## Vinyl Siding

- Vinyl moves with temperature; do not nail tight.
- Center fasteners in slots and leave panels able to slide.
- Leave required expansion gaps at trim/accessories.
- Keep panels unlocked/locked per profile without forcing.
- Use proper starter strip, corner posts, J-channel, utility trim, and undersill trim.
- Flash openings behind accessories; J-channel is not flashing by itself.
- High-wind areas may require specific products and fastening.

## Fiber-Cement Siding

- Heavy, brittle, and dust-producing; cut with dust control.
- Maintain required clearances from grade, roofs, decks, and horizontal surfaces.
- Prime/seal cut ends where manufacturer requires.
- Use approved fasteners and nailing method.
- Manage butt joints with flashing or approved joint treatment.
- Do not install wet or store directly on ground.
- Respect paint/finish requirements and repaint intervals.

## Wood Siding

- Needs back-priming/sealing, especially cut ends and high-exposure areas.
- Maintain clearances and ventilation to dry.
- Use corrosion-resistant fasteners.
- Detail horizontal trim and joints to shed water.
- Avoid end grain exposure and water traps.
- Stain/paint schedule is part of durability.

## Engineered Wood Siding

- Follow manufacturer details exactly for clearances, cut edges, fasteners, joint treatment, and flashing.
- Seal cuts and damage as required.
- Keep off grade, roofs, and concrete.
- Do not install damaged/swollen pieces.
- Store dry and protected.

## Metal Siding

- Expansion, oil canning, corrosion, and trim detailing matter.
- Use compatible fasteners and isolation from dissimilar metals.
- Protect cut edges per manufacturer instructions.
- Maintain straight layout; panel misalignment is highly visible.
- Avoid leaving metal shavings on finished surfaces.

## Stucco and Masonry Veneer Awareness

- Stucco needs drainage, lath, weeps, casing beads, control joints, and correct integration with WRB/flashing.
- Masonry veneer needs an air/drainage cavity, weeps, flashing, ties, lintels, and support.
- Do not block weeps.
- Watch transitions between absorptive claddings and wood trim.
- Cracks, bulges, rust staining, or interior staining can indicate deeper failures.

## Exterior Trim and Band Boards

- Horizontal trim needs cap flashing or a sloped top surface.
- Back-prime or seal cut ends where required.
- Do not create shelves that hold water.
- Use drip caps and kerfs to break surface tension.
- Keep trim proud/flat only if drainage is handled behind it.

## Penetrations

Common penetrations:

- Hose bibbs.
- Exterior outlets and lights.
- Dryer vents.
- Bath/kitchen exhaust.
- HVAC linesets.
- Gas/electric meters.
- Deck ledgers.
- Rail brackets.
- Security cameras.

Each penetration needs blocking as needed, flashing/WRB integration, weatherproof trim/block, sealant, and slope/drainage where applicable.

## Common Failure Modes

- Reverse-lapped WRB or flashing tape.
- Missing pan flashing at windows/doors.
- Missing kickout flashing.
- Siding too close to grade, roof, deck, or concrete.
- Caulk used where metal flashing is needed.
- Over-nailed vinyl or under-nailed fiber cement.
- Horizontal trim without cap flashing.
- Blocked weeps or drainage gaps.
- Corroded fasteners in treated lumber/coastal exposure.
- Poor integration between siding and roofing.

## Field QA Checklist

Before siding is complete:

- WRB continuous and repaired.
- Flashing laps shingle-style.
- Openings flashed with sill, jamb, and head strategy.
- Kickouts installed at roof-wall intersections.
- Clearances verified.
- Fasteners correct and not overdriven.
- Drainage gaps/weep paths open.
- Penetrations blocked, flashed, and sealed.
- Horizontal trim capped or sloped.
- Touch-up, cut-end sealing, and paint requirements completed.
- Photos taken before cladding covers WRB/flashing.
