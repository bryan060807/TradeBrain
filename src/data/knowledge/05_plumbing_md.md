# Plumbing

Plumbing work affects health, sanitation, water damage risk, and inspections. Many plumbing tasks require a licensed plumber depending on jurisdiction. This file is for construction coordination and practical field awareness, not a substitute for local plumbing code or professional design.

## Core Priorities

1. Protect potable water from contamination.
2. Provide reliable drainage with correct slope, venting, and cleanouts.
3. Prevent leaks, freezing, noise, water hammer, and concealed damage.
4. Maintain access to valves, traps, cleanouts, pumps, heaters, and equipment.
5. Coordinate framing so plumbing does not destroy structure.
6. Test before cover-up.

## Rough-In Coordination

Before framing close-in:

- Confirm fixture locations from finished surfaces, not rough framing alone.
- Verify tub/shower size, valve depth, drain location, toilet rough-in, vanity layout, and appliance specs.
- Plan wet walls with enough depth for drains, vents, supplies, insulation, and nail protection.
- Avoid placing large DWV stacks in narrow load-bearing walls unless planned.
- Coordinate joist direction, beams, dropped ceilings, chases, and soffits.
- Keep cleanouts accessible.
- Photograph all rough-ins before insulation/drywall.

## DWV Basics

DWV means drain, waste, and vent. It must move waste water, carry sewer gases safely outdoors, and maintain trap seals.

### Drainage

- Horizontal drains need continuous slope in the correct direction.
- Too little slope causes solids to settle; too much slope can let water outrun solids in some systems.
- Avoid bellies, sags, flat spots, and backfall.
- Support pipe at required intervals and near fittings.
- Use long sweeps and proper drainage fittings; do not use vent-only fittings in drainage flow paths.
- Maintain cleanout access at required locations.

### Traps

- Every fixture needing a trap must have a properly sized trap.
- Trap arms have limits based on pipe size, slope, and code.
- Do not double-trap fixtures.
- Do not create S-traps.
- Protect trap seals from siphonage, evaporation, pressure, and backflow.

### Venting

- Vents protect trap seals and allow drainage air movement.
- Vent sizing, distance, wet venting, circuit venting, island venting, and air admittance valves are code-sensitive.
- Air admittance valves, where allowed, need access and air intake space.
- Vent terminations must meet roof, window, intake, and clearance rules.
- Do not assume an old venting arrangement is legal or functional.

## Pipe Materials

### PVC and ABS DWV

- Use the correct cement/primer system for the material and jurisdiction.
- Dry fit can mislead; pipe seats deeper when cemented.
- Deburr/chamfer cuts.
- Maintain proper slope and support while cement cures.
- Do not mix PVC and ABS without approved transition method.

### Cast Iron

- Good for sound control and fire resistance.
- Heavy; needs proper support and shielded couplings where required.
- Cutting and joining require correct tools and safety.
- Existing cast iron can corrode internally; inspect before tying in.

### Copper Water Lines

- Type and application matter.
- Protect from freezing, corrosion, abrasion, and dissimilar metals.
- Use proper soldering/brazing/press methods.
- Deburr cuts and maintain clean joints.
- Isolate from aggressive concrete/masonry/soil where needed.

### PEX Water Lines

- Follow manufacturer system: PEX-A, PEX-B, expansion, crimp, clamp, press, fittings, and tool calibration.
- Protect from UV exposure.
- Support to limit sag and noise.
- Use bend supports and avoid kinks; repair kinks per manufacturer method.
- Protect at penetrations and from abrasion.
- Maintain clearance from heat sources.

### CPVC

- Use compatible cement and fittings.
- More brittle than PEX; protect from impact and movement.
- Confirm compatibility with firestopping, sealants, spray foam, and other chemicals.

## Water Supply Layout

- Use shutoff valves at fixtures where required and practical.
- Provide access to main shutoff, PRV, backflow devices, filters, softeners, water heater valves, and manifolds.
- Size piping for fixture demand and pressure loss; do not assume old pipe sizes are adequate for new loads.
- Avoid dead legs where possible.
- Insulate hot water lines where required or beneficial.
- Keep water lines out of exterior walls in freeze climates unless protected by design.

## Water Pressure and Hammer

- Excess pressure can damage fixtures and appliances.
- Pressure reducing valves, expansion tanks, check valves, and water hammer arrestors are system-specific.
- Secure pipes to reduce movement.
- Use hammer arrestors where required for quick-closing valves such as dishwashers, clothes washers, and ice makers.

## Fixtures and Rough-In Awareness

### Toilets

- Rough-in is measured from finished wall to center of flange.
- Flange should be set relative to finished floor height per code/manufacturer practice.
- Secure flange to structure.
- Avoid joist conflicts under toilet centerline.
- Maintain side clearances and front clearance.

### Tubs and Showers

- Set units level and fully supported as manufacturer requires.
- Valve depth must account for finished wall thickness.
- Provide backing for valves, shower arms, grab bars, doors, and accessories.
- Waterproofing is separate from tile; tile and grout are not waterproofing.
- Use flood testing where required or good practice for pans.

### Sinks and Vanities

- Coordinate drain/supply height with vanity drawers, backs, shelves, and trap space.
- Wall-hung sinks and vanities need structural backing.
- Keep traps accessible.

### Kitchens and Laundry

- Coordinate sink, dishwasher, disposal, ice maker, pot filler, washer box, standpipe, vent, and appliance specs.
- Dishwasher drains require proper high loop or air gap as locally required.
- Washer boxes need valve access and drain sizing per code.

## Water Heaters and Mechanical Plumbing

- Water heaters require combustion air, venting, clearances, seismic straps where required, temperature/pressure relief discharge, pan/drain where required, expansion control, and service access.
- Tankless heaters require manufacturer-specific venting, gas sizing, condensate handling, and flushing access.
- Do not improvise relief valve piping.
- Gas piping is licensed/safety-critical work.

## Freeze Protection

- Keep supply lines inside conditioned space where possible.
- Air seal penetrations before relying on insulation.
- Insulation slows heat loss; it does not create heat.
- Hose bibbs need freeze-resistant details in cold climates.
- Protect pipes in crawlspaces, garages, attics, exterior walls, and cantilevers.

## Firestopping and Sound

- Penetrations through rated assemblies need approved firestopping.
- Seal annular spaces as required.
- Isolate noisy pipes from framing.
- Cast iron, insulation, resilient supports, and chase design can reduce drain noise.

## Testing and Inspection

- DWV often requires water or air testing depending on code and stage.
- Water supply lines require pressure testing before cover-up.
- Test shower pans before tile.
- Inspect all joints under pressure and after cycling valves.
- Protect rough-ins during drywall, tile, cabinets, and trim.

## Common Failure Modes

- Drain slope wrong or lost due to poor support.
- Trap arm too long or improperly vented.
- Cleanouts buried behind finishes.
- Shower valve set too deep/shallow.
- Tub/shower not supported, causing movement and leaks.
- Water lines in exterior walls freeze.
- Nail/screw punctures from missing protection plates.
- Dishwasher/washer drains improperly arranged.
- PEX exposed to UV or unsupported.
- Relief valve discharge piped incorrectly.

## Field QA Checklist

Before cover-up:

- Fixture centers and finished-wall dimensions confirmed.
- DWV slope verified.
- Traps, vents, and cleanouts accessible/approved.
- Water lines supported and protected.
- Nail plates installed where needed.
- Shutoffs accessible.
- Tub/shower backing installed.
- Firestopping complete where required.
- Pressure/leak tests passed.
- Photos taken before insulation/drywall.
