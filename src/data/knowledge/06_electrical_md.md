# Electrical

Electrical work is life-safety work. Many tasks require a licensed electrician and permit. This file is for construction coordination, inspection awareness, and field communication. Do not advise users to work energized, bypass protection, oversize breakers, defeat grounding, or modify service equipment without qualified/licensed help.

## Core Priorities

1. Keep workers and occupants safe from shock, fire, and arc hazards.
2. Follow the locally adopted electrical code and amendments.
3. Maintain proper circuit sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, GFCI/AFCI protection, box fill, and working clearances.
4. Protect cables/conduits from nails, screws, abrasion, heat, moisture, and physical damage.
5. Coordinate framing, blocking, insulation, cabinets, tile, siding, and equipment access before rough-in.
6. Test before cover-up and again before occupancy.

## Construction Coordination

Before rough-in:

- Confirm panel location, service route, meter location, utility requirements, and working clearance.
- Verify reflected ceiling plan, switch locations, outlet layout, appliance specs, HVAC equipment, low-voltage needs, exterior lighting, and cabinet design.
- Identify dedicated circuits for kitchen, laundry, bath, garage, HVAC, sump, disposal, dishwasher, microwave, refrigerator, range, EV charger, pumps, and large equipment as required.
- Coordinate recessed lights with joists, trusses, ducts, insulation, fire-rated assemblies, and attic access.
- Coordinate exterior penetrations with WRB, siding blocks, flashing, and weatherproof covers.
- Install backing for heavy fixtures, fans, sconces, under-cabinet lights, and low-voltage panels.

## Rough-In Layout

Typical heights vary by project and accessibility requirements. Use plans/specs/local rules.

- Keep switch heights consistent through the project.
- Align receptacles where visible, especially above countertops, backsplashes, trim bands, and wainscot.
- Set boxes proud/flush for the finished wall thickness: drywall, tile, paneling, stone, cabinets, or exterior trim blocks.
- Use extension rings where needed; do not bury boxes behind finishes.
- Do not place boxes where casing, base, crown, cabinets, shower surrounds, mirrors, appliances, or handrails will conflict.

## Cables, Conduit, and Protection

- Use wiring method approved for the location and exposure.
- NM cable, MC cable, EMT, PVC conduit, rigid metal conduit, and flexible conduit have different rules and uses.
- Protect wiring passing through framing where close to the face with nail plates.
- Support and secure cables/conduits per code and manufacturer rules.
- Avoid damaging cable sheathing when pulling through holes.
- Maintain separation from heat sources, sharp metal, flues, and plumbing where required.
- Wet locations require wiring methods and boxes rated for wet use.
- Underground conduit/cable depth and type are code-sensitive.

## Box Fill and Device Boxes

- Box size must account for conductors, devices, internal clamps, equipment grounding conductors, and pigtails as required by code.
- Crowded boxes overheat, damage insulation, and make devices hard to install.
- Use deep boxes where dimmers, smart switches, GFCI devices, splices, or multiple conductors are expected.
- Fan boxes must be rated for fan support.
- Luminaire boxes must support the fixture weight or use listed supports.
- Junction boxes must remain accessible.

## Grounding and Bonding Awareness

- Grounding and bonding are not the same thing, but both are critical.
- Equipment grounding conductors help clear faults.
- Bonding connects metal parts to reduce shock risk.
- Service bonding, grounding electrode systems, subpanels, detached structures, metal water piping, gas piping bonding, pools/spas, and generators are licensed/electrician territory.
- Never remove grounding prongs or bootleg grounds.
- Do not use plumbing pipes as a shortcut for grounding unless explicitly part of a compliant grounding/bonding system.

## GFCI and AFCI Awareness

- GFCI protection reduces shock risk, especially near water, outdoors, garages, basements, crawlspaces, kitchens, baths, laundry, and other code-required areas.
- AFCI protection reduces fire risk from arcing faults and is required in many dwelling areas by modern codes.
- Requirements vary by adopted code edition and local amendments.
- Nuisance tripping should be diagnosed, not bypassed.
- GFCI/AFCI devices and breakers must be wired correctly, including line/load where applicable.

## Receptacles and Switches

- Spacing rules are code-sensitive and occupancy-specific.
- Countertop, island, peninsula, bathroom, laundry, garage, exterior, basement, and appliance receptacles have special requirements.
- Tamper-resistant and weather-resistant devices may be required.
- Exterior receptacles need weatherproof boxes/covers and proper in-use covers where required.
- Switch locations should be intuitive, code-compliant, and coordinated with door swings and trim.
- Three-way/four-way switching, smart switches, and dimmers require correct conductors and box space.

## Lighting and Fans

- Recessed lights must match insulation contact, airtight, damp/wet location, slope ceiling, fire rating, and fixture trim requirements.
- Ceiling fans require fan-rated boxes and proper support.
- Bathroom/shower lights need appropriate damp/wet ratings and GFCI protection where required.
- Under-cabinet lighting needs transformer/driver access and heat management.
- Exterior fixtures need weatherproof mounting blocks and sealed penetrations.

## Service Panels and Equipment

- Panel work is for qualified electricians.
- Maintain required working clearance, dedicated space, labeling, and access.
- Do not bury panels in closets, cabinets, bathrooms, or restricted spaces where prohibited.
- Breaker size must match conductor ampacity and circuit requirements.
- Multi-wire branch circuits, subpanels, neutrals, grounds, bonding screws, feeders, and generators require proper design.
- Label circuits clearly and accurately.

## Low Voltage

Low voltage still needs planning.

- Route data, coax, security, audio, cameras, doorbells, thermostats, and controls before insulation.
- Keep required separation from power wiring.
- Use conduit/smurf tube for future pulls where useful.
- Provide backing and power for TVs, access points, shades, speakers, and cameras.
- Centralize network panels with ventilation and service access.

## Firestopping and Air Sealing

- Electrical penetrations through rated assemblies require approved firestopping.
- Top/bottom plate penetrations may need fireblocking/air sealing.
- Airtight boxes or sealing details may be needed for energy performance.
- Do not foam or caulk in a way that violates box/device listing or overheats equipment.

## Remodel Work

- Assume old wiring may be unsafe until verified.
- Knob-and-tube, aluminum branch wiring, cloth wiring, ungrounded systems, overloaded panels, DIY splices, and buried junctions need qualified evaluation.
- Use circuit tracing before demolition.
- Lock out/tag out when possible.
- Do not rely on a non-contact tester alone for final proof of de-energization.

## Common Failure Modes

- Boxes set wrong for tile/cabinet/backsplash thickness.
- Receptacles hidden behind appliances or trim.
- Cable too close to framing face without nail plate.
- Junction box buried behind drywall.
- Wrong box for ceiling fan.
- Overfilled boxes.
- Missing GFCI/AFCI where required.
- Neutrals shared or switched incorrectly.
- Exterior penetrations not flashed/sealed.
- Panel circuits mislabeled.

## Field QA Checklist

Before cover-up:

- Box locations checked against plans, cabinets, tile, trim, appliances, and door swings.
- Cables/conduits secured and protected.
- Nail plates installed.
- Box fill adequate.
- Fan/heavy fixture boxes rated and supported.
- Junction boxes accessible.
- Firestopping/air sealing complete where required.
- Exterior boxes weatherproofed and integrated with siding/WRB.
- Rough electrical inspection passed.
- Photos taken before insulation/drywall.
