# Concrete

Concrete work is unforgiving because mistakes become heavy, expensive, and difficult to access. Durable concrete depends on subgrade prep, drainage, formwork, reinforcement placement, mix suitability, placement, finishing timing, curing, and joint layout.

## Core Priorities

1. Stable, compacted subgrade.
2. Proper drainage and moisture control.
3. Correct forms, elevations, slopes, and dimensions.
4. Reinforcement placed where designed, not lying on the ground.
5. Mix suited to exposure, strength, finish, and placement conditions.
6. Proper consolidation, finishing, curing, and jointing.
7. Protection from freezing, rapid drying, rain, and premature loading.

## Site Prep and Subgrade

- Strip organic material, roots, loose fill, mud, and debris.
- Compact subgrade and base in lifts where fill is required.
- Soft spots must be removed and replaced, not hidden.
- Manage water before placement; concrete over mud performs poorly.
- Use granular base where specified or needed for drainage and support.
- Verify elevations, slopes, step-downs, thickened edges, trenches, sleeves, and embeds before pour.

## Drainage and Moisture

- Slope exterior slabs away from buildings.
- Keep water from collecting at foundation walls, stoops, garage entries, and flatwork.
- Use capillary breaks and vapor retarders where required, especially under interior slabs and conditioned spaces.
- Lap and tape vapor retarder seams where specified.
- Protect vapor barrier from punctures and repair damage before placement.
- Coordinate underslab plumbing/electrical before base and vapor barrier are complete.

## Forms

- Forms must hold line, grade, dimensions, and pressure during placement.
- Stake and brace forms well; wet concrete moves weak forms.
- Check diagonals, elevations, slopes, reveal heights, and door/garage transitions.
- Apply form release where appropriate.
- Chamfer or edge exposed corners where needed.
- Verify anchor bolts, hold-downs, sleeves, blockouts, drains, and embeds before the truck arrives.

## Reinforcement

- Rebar and welded wire reinforcement must be placed at the designed height with chairs/supports.
- Mesh on the ground does little; it must be lifted or chaired into position.
- Reinforcement controls crack width and structural capacity; it does not prevent all cracking.
- Maintain cover to soil, weather, and formed surfaces per design/code.
- Lap lengths, hooks, dowels, and bar sizes are design-sensitive.
- Avoid cutting reinforcement without approval.

## Concrete Mix Awareness

Concrete mix should match application:

- Footings and walls.
- Interior slabs.
- Exterior slabs exposed to freeze-thaw.
- Driveways and garage slabs.
- Pumped concrete.
- Decorative/stamped concrete.
- High-early-strength or cold-weather work.

Important mix factors:

- Compressive strength.
- Slump/workability.
- Water-cement ratio.
- Air entrainment for freeze-thaw exposure.
- Aggregate size.
- Admixtures.
- Set time.

Do not add water casually on site. Extra water can reduce strength, increase shrinkage, increase dusting/scaling, and worsen cracking.

## Placement

- Place concrete close to final location; avoid excessive dragging and segregation.
- Consolidate around forms, reinforcement, corners, blockouts, and penetrations.
- Do not over-vibrate or segregate mix.
- Screed to correct elevation and slope.
- Bull float promptly to embed aggregate and flatten ridges.
- Maintain edge forms until concrete can hold shape.

## Finishing

- Timing matters more than force.
- Do not finish bleed water into the surface.
- Exterior concrete usually needs a slip-resistant finish such as broom texture.
- Hard troweling air-entrained exterior concrete can contribute to scaling.
- Edge and joint at proper time.
- Protect from rain during finishing.
- Match finish to use: garage, driveway, patio, basement, polished, tile-ready, drain slope, ADA surface.

## Curing

Curing keeps moisture and temperature conditions favorable so concrete can gain strength and durability.

- Start curing after finishing when surface will not be damaged.
- Use curing compound, wet curing, coverings, or other approved method.
- Protect from rapid drying due to sun, wind, low humidity, or heat.
- Protect from freezing in cold weather.
- Avoid early heavy loads, traffic, framing loads, or equipment until concrete has sufficient strength.

## Control Joints and Cracking

Concrete cracks. Good jointing controls where cracks occur.

- Sawcut or tool joints at proper spacing and timing.
- Joint depth and layout must be appropriate for slab thickness and geometry.
- Avoid re-entrant corners without joints; inside corners crack.
- Use isolation joints where slabs meet walls, columns, footings, stoops, pipes, and other fixed elements.
- Plan joint layout with finish appearance and load paths.
- Random cracking is often caused by poor joint timing, bad layout, high shrinkage, poor curing, restraint, or subgrade movement.

## Footings

- Footing size, depth, reinforcement, and frost depth are code/engineer-sensitive.
- Place on undisturbed soil or properly compacted engineered fill.
- Keep water and mud out of footing trenches.
- Step footings correctly on slopes.
- Locate keyways, dowels, anchor bolts, and wall forms accurately.
- Verify setbacks, dimensions, and elevations before pour.

## Foundation Walls

- Wall thickness, reinforcement, height, backfill, waterproofing, drainage, and bracing are design-sensitive.
- Do not backfill before walls/floors are braced and concrete has adequate strength.
- Provide footing drains, dampproofing/waterproofing, drainage board, and free-draining backfill where required.
- Protect waterproofing from damage during backfill.
- Sleeve penetrations and seal them properly.
- Honeycombing, cold joints, excessive cracking, or bowing require evaluation.

## Slabs-on-Grade

- Confirm subgrade, base, vapor barrier, insulation, thickened edges, reinforcement, sleeves, drains, and anchor locations.
- Interior slabs should be flat enough for flooring or equipment requirements.
- Garage slabs need slope to door/drain as designed.
- Control joints should be laid out before pouring.
- Coordinate sawcut timing; waiting too long invites random cracks.

## Exterior Flatwork

- Slope away from structures.
- Use air-entrained mix in freeze-thaw climates.
- Avoid overworking surface or adding water during finishing.
- Provide isolation at foundation walls, steps, columns, and existing slabs.
- Tool/saw control joints with a clean pattern.
- Consider dowels or thickened edges where slabs meet and differential movement matters.
- Manage deicing salt exposure and first-winter protection.

## Anchors and Embeds

- Anchor bolts, hold-downs, post bases, sleeves, drains, and conduit must be placed accurately.
- Secure embeds so they do not move during placement.
- Post-installed anchors require correct hole diameter, depth, cleaning, edge distance, spacing, adhesive/set time, and inspection where required.
- Do not relocate structural anchors without approval.

## Cold and Hot Weather

### Cold Weather

- Frozen subgrade is unacceptable for most placements.
- Protect concrete from freezing until it gains sufficient strength.
- Use blankets, heated enclosures, warm mix, accelerators, or schedule changes as needed.
- Avoid calcium chloride where it conflicts with reinforcement, embeds, flooring, or specs.

### Hot Weather

- Heat, wind, and low humidity accelerate moisture loss and set time.
- Use windbreaks, shade, fogging, chilled water, retarder, or schedule early pours when appropriate.
- Prevent plastic shrinkage cracking.
- Cure aggressively.

## Common Failure Modes

- Pouring over uncompacted fill or mud.
- Reinforcement lying at bottom of slab.
- No vapor barrier under moisture-sensitive floors.
- Adding too much water at truck.
- Finishing bleed water into surface.
- Late sawcuts causing random cracks.
- Missing isolation joints at fixed objects.
- Exterior slab flat or sloped toward building.
- No curing or poor cold-weather protection.
- Backfilling foundation walls too early.
- Anchor bolts misplaced.

## Field QA Checklist

Before pour:

- Subgrade compacted and dry/stable.
- Forms braced, straight, square, and at correct elevation.
- Base, vapor barrier, insulation, sleeves, and drains complete.
- Reinforcement size, spacing, lap, cover, and chairs verified.
- Anchor bolts, embeds, blockouts, and hold-downs located.
- Access, pump, chute, crew, tools, curing materials, and weather plan ready.

During/after pour:

- Slump/workability acceptable without excessive added water.
- Consolidation complete.
- Finish timing correct; bleed water not trapped.
- Joints cut/tooling completed on schedule.
- Curing started and maintained.
- Protection from weather and traffic installed.
- Photos and batch tickets saved.
